The Legend of the Golem of Prague & Autonomous Agents

Why an ancient 16th-century Jewish myth is the exact mental model needed for 21st-century decentralized AI.

In 16th-century Prague, Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel molded clay from the banks of the Vltava River to create the Golem—a mechanical protector designed to defend the vulnerable from persecution. The creature was infused with life by inscribing the Hebrew word EMET (אמת — Truth) upon its forehead.

The Golem had no speech, no pride, and no hesitation. It simply executed the commands it was given with terrifying physical efficiency. But it possessed no moral judgment. Left unchecked without continuous truth, the Golem would mistake its orders, run wild in the streets, and threaten the very people it was built to protect.

To stop the creature before disaster struck, the Rabbi would erase the first Hebrew letter (Aleph), transforming EMET into MET (מת — Dead / Halt). Deprived of truth, the Golem instantly collapsed back into harmless dust.

"We have built modern Golems: autonomous AI trading agents that negotiate, borrow, and settle across blockchains. When an agent executes blindly on unverified notifications, it destroys treasuries. EMET is the inscription of truth on NEAR."

By anchoring every agent action in NEAR's decentralized MPC network, EMET ensures that an agent only moves when truth is mathematically proven. When a transaction fails or an exploit is attempted, the kernel erases the intent, halts the operation, and refunds the treasury immediately.
